# Volunteer repair cafe: first-month project design

A neighborhood group wants to run a monthly repair cafe where volunteers help residents diagnose small household items. The group has five volunteers, a donated community room for one four-hour Saturday session each month, and no budget for a custom app. Most residents will arrive without registering. The team wants a paper-first process that can later be copied into a shared spreadsheet.

Design a practical first-month operating approach. The most urgent piece is intake and triage: how a resident describes an item, how the team decides whether it is safe and in scope, how an item gets assigned to a volunteer, and how the outcome is recorded. Keep the process friendly and quick for visitors, while giving volunteers a clear way to refuse unsafe work. The cafe does not repair mains-powered appliances, lithium battery packs, or items that require specialized certification. Volunteers may explain a likely problem, but should avoid promising a successful repair.

The final approach should let the group run a pilot with ordinary paper supplies, a shared spreadsheet after the event, and a short volunteer briefing. Include what the pilot should measure and what the group should review before deciding whether to repeat or expand it. Prefer one coherent workflow over a long menu of possible systems.
